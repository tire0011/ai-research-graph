## Original questions and how they connect

1. Why can training with several languages improve abilities in languages that were not directly trained? Does more data help or is there a shared learning mechanism?
2. Can transformations of weights and activations (spectral, geometric, dynamical or other) expose mechanisms that ordinary evaluation misses?
3. Have similar analyses already been tried by other fields or by major LLM researchers? What remains genuinely unknown?
4. What can we learn from neuroscience and the biology of learning, without assuming that transformers duplicate biological brains?
5. Can our existing cross-platform research graph help generate **research-worthy, falsifiable** ideas from observations and contradictions rather than simply finding related papers?
6. How do we preserve the **evolution of questions** without changing the overall goal whenever a new curiosity emerges?
7. Can we freeze knowledge at **2023-12-31**, propose what research should discover, and check the independent 2024–2026 record afterward?

## Working synthesis, not an established claim

A research-discovery system should connect *human questions*, *mechanistic explanations from multiple fields*, *documented evidence and counterevidence*, *falsifiable hypotheses*, and *minimal experiments*. Different questions are paths through the same research program, not independent pivots. The graph's existing source and confidence discipline is a starting point, not evidence that the new approach works.

We need **recurrent checkpoints**: capture meaningful thought snapshots and later revisit, correct, combine, or extend them through **new versioned synthesis**. Past reasoning and failures remain accessible; do not overwrite them.

The historical replay experiment tests whether the resulting method contributes value beyond simple search or an LLM using the same sources. The trial should not assume success.

## What is established vs uncertain

- **Established for this repository:** the existing graph links bounded, sourced research artifacts, uses different evidence levels, and includes small intervention and retrieval probes; see README and current evaluation documents.
- **Known research precedents to investigate:** multilingual transfer; representation geometry / spectral analysis; training dynamics; mechanistic interpretability; neuroscience-inspired replay and consolidation; graph-based scientific hypothesis generation. Being inspired by these areas does **not** establish novelty.
- **Unverified proposals:** the checkpoint/synthesis workflow improves idea retention; cross-disciplinary mechanistic connections produce higher-quality hypotheses; the graph beats simple source-following search; any specific multilingual-transfer mechanism.
- **Main risk:** modern LLMs may already know future results of a historical 2023 cutoff. Date-filtered retrieval does not erase this contamination.
