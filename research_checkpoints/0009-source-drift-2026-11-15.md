## Why it matters to the overall research graph

The research graph stores dated ideas, evidence and possible collaborations. A research decision can become stale **without any new graph connection**: the upstream implementation may merge, an experiment may finish or new evidence may contradict a recommendation. Repeating old advice wastes attention and can duplicate engineering.

**This is a concrete quality failure mode**, distinct from graph search relevance or automatic discovery. The smallest useful technical improvement was a bounded, read-only original-source check of critical assumptions **just before making a recommendation**.

## New decision rule

Every actionable candidate should carry a *time-indexed critical assumption*:

`candidate → dated claim (PR open) → original-source recheck (merged) → review recommendation again`

- Do not overwrite the original claim; keep earlier and later observations linked.
- Treat `open`, `closed without merge` and `merged` separately.
- Verify the specific source identity; a failed API request is **unknown**, not an unchanged record.
- Only propose an action after its *actual unmet need*, metric compatibility, and cost are re-verified.
- No automated messages, PRs, training jobs or code reuse. Independent replication remains a legitimate reason for intentional duplication.

## What this does NOT prove

- A direct GitHub status check can find the same information. **No advantage of graph structure over text/source search was demonstrated.**
- No researcher time, saved GPU-hours, changed maintainer decision or accepted cross-project handoff was measured.
- This tool currently audits **PR state only**, not scientific claims, issue comments, research-paper results or current reproducibility.
- A weekly source-watch artifact is an aid to human review, not a scientific discovery system or continuous background agent in this conversation.

## Next high-information test

Select one *genuinely pending* research decision before examining its sources. Require the **same original evidence** for graph-assisted and source-first reviewers, and an independent check of (1) decision correctness, (2) unneeded work avoided, (3) review effort. If the simpler method reaches the same result, preserve that negative finding and stop extending the graph.

The wider mission remains one connected program: avoid redundant work, maximize low-cost leverage, coordinate research, discover falsifiable hypotheses, preserve discoveries and failures across checkpoints.
